Marketing mix modeling · interactive

How a marketing mix model actually decides

Five things you can pull on. Each one isolates a single decision the model makes, so you can push it until it breaks and see why the answer changes. Everything runs in your browser — no data leaves the page, and every number is illustrative.

01

Trace one dollar

Follow a single dollar through adstock, saturation, and the channel coefficient — next to the same pipeline with that channel zeroed out. The gap between them is the attributed outcome.

→ why ROI is a difference, not a share 02

Read a response curve

Reshape one channel's saturation curve and drag along it. The curve goes dashed past the spend you've actually observed — beyond that line the model is extrapolating on functional form alone.

→ where your data ends and guessing starts 03

Pick what to grow

One spend decision, four outcomes — pipeline dollars, closed-won, qualified leads, stage transitions. Different units, different answers. Starve one and watch it refuse to answer at all.

→ why a count ROI can't be compared to a dollar ROI 04

Split the budget

Drag channel shares against a fixed total, then optimise. The marginal returns converge to equal across channels — which is the whole reason you don't just pour everything into the highest-ROI one.

→ where the next dollar should go 05

Decide if it ships

Drive the release gates yourself — sampler convergence, agreement with a real lift test, and whether the ranking survives perturbed data. Any one of them can block the result.
